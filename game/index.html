Under the hood

How it’s made

No hand-built levels. The city is generated from public data and rendered live in your browser with three.js.

  1. The map

    Overpass API → OpenStreetMap ways → map.json

    A build script pulls buildings with their heights and levels, roads and their widths, parks, piers, rail lines, street trees, traffic signals, crossings, lamps and points of interest for the northeast of the city, then flattens it all into a compact file of about 40,000 building footprints and 17,600 road segments.

  2. The ground

    AWS Terrain Tiles (USGS 3DEP) → height field

    Elevation tiles are stitched into a height field so every street climbs and drops exactly as it does in real life. Buildings sit on the slope, roads drape over it, and your speed responds to the grade.

  3. The buildings

    footprint + height → procedural facades

    Footprints are extruded and dressed in procedural facades: floors, window bays, shopfronts and curtain-wall glass, with angled bay windows and bracketed cornices on the row houses. Windows use interior mapping, a shader trick that fakes a real room behind each pane, so the city looks inhabited from any angle.

  4. The light

    three.js → cinematic renderer

    Cascaded shadow maps keep shadows sharp up close and far away. N8AO adds ambient occlusion in the corners, bloom makes lights glow, AgX tone mapping keeps sunsets from clipping, and height fog gives the Bay its depth.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. No ads, no account, no in-app purchases. It’s a hobby project.

Does it work on my phone?

It runs in any modern browser with WebGL 2. A laptop or desktop with a keyboard gives the best experience; touch and gamepad controls are planned.

How much of San Francisco is in it?

The northeast of the city: from SoMa and Mission Bay up through downtown, Chinatown, North Beach, Russian and Nob Hill, the Marina and the Presidio to the Golden Gate Bridge, plus Alcatraz out in the Bay.

My building looks wrong. Why?

Buildings come straight from OpenStreetMap. If a height or shape is off, the map probably says so too, and anyone can fix it on OpenStreetMap. The fix will show up the next time the city is rebuilt.

Is it accurate enough to plan a bike route?

Roughly: the hills and streets are real, but it’s a game, not a navigation app. Don’t ride the Embarcadero as a sea lion in real life.
